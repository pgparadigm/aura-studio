# Aura Studio — sonic families

Aura ships six **sonic families**. Each is a complete system: a tempo, a key and mode, a chord
vocabulary, a drum pattern, a bass behaviour, a set of voices, and **five named controls of its own**.

They came out of a research programme into how a small number of very different records were actually
made — the internal dossier is `research/YE-PRODUCTION-RESEARCH.md`. What crossed over is
**technique**: where the weight sits in a bar, how a section opens, what gets taken away, how a chord
bed makes room for a voice. What did not cross over, and never will, is any recording, melody,
arrangement, lyric, sample, name, likeness or association.

**No artist, album or song name appears anywhere a user can see it.** They appear in the research
dossier, which is an internal engineering document, and nowhere else. Aura is not a "make a record
that sounds like someone" product and must never be marketed as one.

---

## What is safe to translate, and what is not

| Safe — technique and workflow | Not safe — expression |
|---|---|
| Where the kick sits against the backbeat | A specific drum pattern lifted from a record |
| Transposing a chord bed up so it sits above a singer's range | A specific melody or topline |
| Chopping a sound and re-triggering it unevenly so it reads as played | Anyone else's recording, chopped or not |
| Capping how many parts can sound at once | A specific arrangement |
| Ending on a held cadence rather than a fade | A specific chord progression as performed on a record |
| Recording more than you need, then removing most of it | A sample, cleared or otherwise |
| Keeping every version of a song you publish | An artist's name, likeness, or an implied endorsement |

A chord *progression in scale degrees* is a common musical building block and is used freely here. A
*performance* of one is not. Aura generates everything it plays from oscillators and noise; it ships no
sample content whatsoever.

## The six

### Soul Blueprint
> *A warm, chopped chord bed that lifts into your range — then gets out of the way so you can sing over it.*

92 BPM · A minor · chords `i – VI – III – VII` · Rhodes-style chord voice · sub bass · swung 16ths.

| Control | What it does | What it must never do |
|---|---|---|
| **Chop** | How many times the chord re-triggers in a bar, 1 → 8, in an uneven order above four so it reads as played rather than as a stutter | Change chord identity, key, or chop the bass or the voice |
| **Soul** | Adds the diatonic 7th by moving to the Rhodes voice | Introduce a note outside the key |
| **Swing** | How far behind the beat the offbeats sit | Swing the kick or the bass — the floor stays square |
| **Warmth** | Record level: the top rolls off and the middle comes forward | Touch the vocal bus |
| **Lift** | Transposes chords and melody up to an octave into the singer's range | Transpose the bass or the drums |

*Descends from:* a loop used as a scaffold rather than a backing; transposition in service of the
singer; the chop as a performance.

### Stadium Ascension
> *A verse that leaves you room, and a chorus that opens like a room three times the size.*

104 BPM · C major · chords `I – vi – IV – V` · pluck into pad · sub bass on roots only · near-straight.

| Control | What it does | What it must never do |
|---|---|---|
| **Scale** | How big the chorus feels — reverb and space open together | Widen anything below 120 Hz |
| **Motion** | How busy the chords are, from held blocks to 16ths | Alter chord identity or order |
| **Glow** | Bright and forward, or dark and behind | Apply to the bass or the voice |
| **Drive** | Swaps the verse kick for the chorus kick | Put the chorus kick into a verse |
| **Lift** | Raises the final section | Transpose mid-section, or more than a whole step |

*Descends from:* the section switch carried by the kick alone; a bass that never moves so the drums do
all the work; layers that appear in exactly one section.

### Maximal Opus
> *Record more than you need, then keep six things. It will sound enormous because of what you threw away.*

84 BPM · E phrygian · chords `i – ♭II – i – ♭VII` · stacked pad and piano · long sub · half-time backbeat.

| Control | What it does | What it must never do |
|---|---|---|
| **Layers** | A hard cap of six parts at once | Exceed six, or silently drop one to make room |
| **Orchestra** | How wide the chord stack spreads | Fill the band where the singer lives |
| **Drama** | How much quieter the verses are | Jump more than 9 dB inside a bar |
| **Contrast** | How much happens in only one section | Make the chords or bass exclusive — they are the spine |
| **Finale** | The ending | **Never fade out.** Always end on a held cadence |

*Descends from:* generating far more material than is kept, and separating the making from the
choosing; a ceiling on simultaneous parts as a style rule rather than a limitation.

### Living Draft
> *Start with almost nothing. Change one thing at a time and keep every version.*

96 BPM · D dorian · chords `i – III`, two bars each · wide piano · very long sub · nearly straight.

| Control | What it does | What it must never do |
|---|---|---|
| **Evolve** | How much changes each time a part comes back | Change key, tempo or chord roots; change anything in the first 8 bars |
| **Space** | Takes things away | Remove the chord root — the singer needs a pitch reference |
| **Pulse** | How much drum there is | Force drums on. **Zero drums is a finished answer** |
| **Rise** | One slow opening across the whole song | Reset mid-song, or sweep the bass or the voice |
| **Revision** | How much of a change is kept as its own version | Silently overwrite or discard a version |

*Descends from:* a record treated as revisable after release, and modular sections that can be
replaced without rebuilding the song.

### Confessional Minimal
> *Almost nothing behind you. Write it today, sing it today, and let nothing hide the take.*

72 BPM · A minor · two chords, eight bars each · piano, root and fifth · dead straight · the driest
system Aura ships.

| Control | What it does | What it must never do |
|---|---|---|
| **Intimacy** | How close and dry it sounds | Push the voice back |
| **Tension** | Adds the note that makes it ache | Leave the key |
| **Space** | Takes things away from behind the singer | Reduce the vocal |
| **Release** | How long the chords ring | Cover the take |
| **Truth** | Leaves the take as it was sung | Add processing the singer did not ask for |

*Descends from:* a short, fast, sparse record where the arrangement gets out of the way; abrupt
contrast used instead of transition.

### Future Monolith
> *Heavy, still and wide. One chord that does not move, and a lot of room around your voice.*

140 BPM (half-time feel) · E phrygian · a near-static drone · filtered pad, root and fifth, no thirds ·
808 bass · straight · gated space.

| Control | What it does | What it must never do |
|---|---|---|
| **Weight** | How much low end sits under everything | Let the low end swamp the voice |
| **Pulse** | How much drum there is | Force drums on |
| **Fracture** | How broken up the top is | Fill the band where the singer lives |
| **Haze** | How much is hidden behind the filter | Filter the voice |
| **Resolve** | Whether the chord ever moves | Move the root without being asked |

*Descends from:* near-static harmony where the movement is textural; weight and space used as the
structure rather than as an effect.
**Note:** this family's musical definition was held back until the source audit was finished, because
the record it draws from has multiple versions and disputed credits. What is encoded here is only what
the research could actually establish. See the dossier's date-stamped section.

## Rules for anyone extending this

1. **Never name the source.** Not in a label, a tooltip, a preset name, a commit message a user can
   see, or marketing.
2. **Never ship audio you did not generate.** Every sound is synthesised at runtime. No samples.
3. **Encode the technique, not the piece.** "The backbeat carries the section change" is a technique.
   A specific bar of a specific record is not.
4. **A control must do something real.** Every control here writes a parameter the project keeps, so a
   move saves, exports and undoes as one step. If a control cannot be implemented honestly, it is not
   shown — a knob that does nothing is worse than a missing feature.
5. **The "must never" column is not decoration.** Each entry is there because breaking it makes the
   result unsingable — the bass gets transposed, the voice gets buried, the chorus loses its centre.
